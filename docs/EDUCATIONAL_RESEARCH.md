Number Lab is a project response to curiosity, not an empirically proven intervention. Keep exploration unscored and mathematical operations exact. Richer concepts can be encountered without permanently damaging practice evidence or forcing inferred prerequisites.

The authoritative clarification makes geometry/spatial reasoning, measurement and exponentiation/roots planned first-class domains. Research supports treating early spatial and measurement ideas seriously, while powers/root progressions remain an educator-reviewed architecture hypothesis rather than a claimed empirical sequence for six-year-olds. Connect square arrays, multiplication, powers and inverse roots through meaningful representations. Do not assume exposure to advanced notation demonstrates understanding or that strength in arithmetic transfers automatically to geometry.

## Disagreement and uncertainty

IES's 2021 guide [E04] rates timed activities as one fluency-building intervention with strong evidence, and discusses accuracy/anxiety in its teacher-led context. Therefore **do not claim research proves all timed work harmful**. This project's authoritative policy is untimed ordinary play and no speed-based mastery/access gates. A later optional private fluency mode would require a specific educational/accessibility decision.

Concrete-to-representational-to-abstract approaches can help connect meaning, but EEF's parallel-development guidance resists a rigid universal ladder. Hints, representation switching and practice scheduling must remain adaptable. Motivation findings are context-dependent and contested; the ban on manipulative streaks/FOMO/ads is project policy regardless of uncertain causal evidence.

Productive-failure research involving adolescents cannot justify forcing young children into frustration. Child-controlled help, examples, skip and stop are conservative recommendations. No reaction time, error pattern or parent anecdote justifies medical/intelligence claims.

## Learner model evidence

The original knowledge-tracing paper [E10] models probabilities per rule in a programming tutor; it is not calibration for early mathematics. Parameter-estimation research [E11] describes identifiability/fitting pitfalls. Publisher/citation year labels for the original paper differ (1994/1995); title/DOI is the stable identity here.

Thresholds, weighted evidence, deterministic states, Bayesian-style estimates and Elo-like ordering are compared in [adaptive model](ADAPTIVE_LEARNING_MODEL.md). Their limitations here are architectural judgments; no comparative trial establishes the winner for this app. Select the simplest auditable rule model, with finite-concept attainability, assistance provenance, uncertainty and independent scope. Its numeric rules are hypotheses requiring educator review and synthetic simulation before consented trials.

Full original Elo-like Maths Garden paper details were not successfully verified in this bounded pass; do not cite remembered timing/efficacy details as facts. Item difficulty calibration and multidimensional representation concerns are sufficient design reasons to defer a fitted rating system.

## Validation agenda

Before real-child play, review graph/prerequisites, family evidence diversity and hints with educators/mathematicians; validate accessible and spoken task meaning with native-language/accessibility reviewers. Later consented small usability sessions should ask whether children understand invitations/help, can stop, enjoy exploration and face avoidable frustration. Assess learning outcomes separately from retention/playtime. Do not collect a full clickstream “just in case.” Report observations and limits; never advertise effectiveness from a few family sessions.
